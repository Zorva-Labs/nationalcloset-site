Rail vs. Modular vs. Custom Wall vs. Custom Floor Closet Systems

Shopping for a closet, you'll run into four kinds of systems — rail, modular, custom wall-mounted and custom floor-based. Rail and modular are the off-the-shelf options. The two we build are the custom ones — cabinetry made to the exact inch of your space, in furniture-grade materials, and priced well below the national brands. Here's how all four compare, and how to choose.

When you start pricing closets, you'll meet four kinds of systems. Two are off-the-shelf — rail and modular — and two are truly custom — custom wall-mounted and custom floor-based. The custom pair is what National Closet Company builds: cabinetry made to the exact inch of your space in furniture-grade laminate and thermally-fused melamine, and priced well below the national custom brands. Here's the honest breakdown of all four.

The one-minute version

  • Rail (wall-mounted track): the big-box option — wire or laminate shelves that clip to a wall-mounted track. Adjustable, but limited in looks and capacity, and not built to your space. And don't assume it's the cheap choice: rail systems like The Container Store's Elfa are usually more expensive than our custom cabinetry once you add all the components — for a system that still has that open, utilitarian, garage-shelving look.
  • Modular (freestanding): pre-made cabinet units you set side by side. More furniture-like than rail, but it's not custom — you're fitting standard-width boxes into a non-standard closet, so you can end up with large gaps to fill at the ends and around the units. Modular pieces also don't share vertical panels: where two units meet you get two side panels back-to-back (a doubled-up seam), much like assembling separate IKEA cabinets next to each other, instead of one continuous built-in run. And it's no bargain either — for the same design, modular usually costs the same or more than a true custom closet.
  • Custom wall-mounted (floating) — we build this: furniture-grade cabinetry built to your exact space that hangs off the wall with the floor open underneath. A lighter, modern look that makes the room feel bigger and is easy to clean under.
  • Custom floor-based (floor-standing) — we build this: furniture-grade cabinetry built to your exact space that sits on the floor with a toe kick, like fine built-in furniture. A substantial, high-capacity feel that supports islands and seating.

Between our two custom lines, neither is better than the other — it's a matter of the look you prefer and your room. (A carpeted room, for example, often suits a floor-based system.) You can even mix the two in one closet. We walk through it in detail in our custom wall-mounted vs. custom floor-based guide.

Custom doesn't have to cost more

The assumption is that "custom" means expensive. It doesn't have to. Neither off-the-shelf route actually saves you money for a comparable design: modular systems typically cost the same or more than a true custom closet, and rail systems like a fully loaded Elfa wall are frequently more expensive than our custom cabinetry outright — yet both are built from standard parts that rarely fit your closet perfectly, leaving gaps at the ends and doubled-up panels where units meet. The national custom brands like California Closets and Closets by Design then charge a premium for the built-to-fit version. National Closet Company sits in between on purpose: true custom cabinetry — wall-mounted or floor-based — at a price normal families can afford. Because it's built as one continuous run, custom fills the space wall-to-wall with no gaps and shares vertical panels between sections instead of doubling them — so you often get more usable storage for the money, with the exact fit and furniture-grade finish and none of the luxury-brand markup.

The one genuine upside of rail and modular is that a handy homeowner can buy and install them themselves — they're really DIY systems. But if you're hiring the work out anyway, you're paying for standard parts and a compromised fit at a price that often meets or beats true custom.

Rail and modular make sense for the DIY installer. If someone else is building it, custom wall or custom floor gives you a better closet — a perfect fit and a furniture-grade finish — usually for the same money or less.

The two we build — and how to pick

Start with one question: are you installing it yourself? If you genuinely want a DIY project, rail or modular is your lane. If someone's installing it for you, go custom — and then the only real question left is wall-mounted or floor-based. Choose custom wall-mounted for a lighter, modern look with the floor open underneath — great for making a smaller room feel bigger and easy to clean under. Choose custom floor-based for a substantial, built-in-furniture feel with the highest capacity and support for islands and seating — often the better call in carpeted rooms. Neither performs better; it's your preference and your room. Our wall-mounted vs. floor-based guide covers it in full, and you can check real budgets in the custom closet cost guide or compare us to the big names in the California Closets vs. local guide.
