How Much Does a Contractor Charge to Build a Closet?

If you're searching for what a contractor charges to build a closet, the honest answer is: it depends on whether you're framing a brand-new closet from scratch or outfitting one that already exists. Here in Middle Tennessee, those are two very different jobs with very different price tags. We'll break down real numbers, realistic timelines, and when hiring a general contractor makes sense versus calling a dedicated custom closet company.

What a contractor actually does when they "build a closet"

When people say they want a contractor to build a closet, they usually mean one of two things. The first is true construction: creating a brand-new closet where none exists. That means framing walls with lumber, hanging and finishing drywall, adding a door, running electrical for a light or outlet, and painting. A general contractor or carpenter is exactly the right person for that work, because it's structural and it changes the footprint of your home.

The second thing people mean is outfitting a closet: adding shelves, rods, drawers, and organization to a space that already has four walls and a door. This is on-site carpentry, and while a contractor can do it, it's rarely where they do their best work or give you the best value. That's the job a dedicated custom closet company was built for.

Typical cost ranges in Middle Tennessee

Prices vary with your home, your finishes, and how much of the work is structural. As a general guide for the Nashville area and surrounding towns like Gallatin, Hendersonville, and Franklin, here's what we typically see:

A few things drive those numbers: whether walls and a door already exist, whether electrical is involved, the quality of materials, and how much labor happens on-site. On-site carpentry is slow and dusty because everything is measured, cut, and finished in your home. A general contractor also often subcontracts the drywall, paint, and electrical, and each of those trades adds coordination time to the schedule. For a deeper breakdown of pricing on the finished-closet side, see our guide on what a custom closet actually costs.

When a contractor genuinely makes sense

Hiring a general contractor or carpenter is the right call when the project involves real construction. Reach for a contractor when:

  • You need a brand-new closet framed where there's currently open wall or an unused nook.
  • The work is structural — moving a wall, altering a load-bearing area, or changing a room's layout.
  • You're mid-renovation and a contractor is already on-site handling drywall, flooring, and paint anyway.
  • You need a door, electrical, or HVAC added as part of the closet.

In those cases, framing the space is the job, and a contractor does it well. What a contractor usually won't give you is a smart, space-maximizing interior. Most will hang a single rod and a shelf, or bill by the hour to build basic shelving on-site. That's where the second half of this decision comes in.

Contractor vs. custom closet company: the honest comparison

If you're weighing a general contractor against a custom closet company, the deciding question is simple: does the closet space already exist? If you need walls built, hire a contractor. If the walls are already there and you want the inside to work harder, a custom closet company is faster, cleaner, and usually a better value. Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to hire a contractor or a custom closet company?
It depends on the job. If you need a brand-new closet framed from scratch, a contractor is the right and often more economical choice for that structural work. If your closet already exists and you just want it outfitted, a custom closet company is usually the better value — one all-inclusive price, furniture-grade materials, and about a one-day install instead of a multi-week schedule.

How long does it take to build a closet?
Framing a brand-new closet — drywall, door, paint, and any electrical — typically takes one to four weeks because multiple trades are involved. Outfitting an existing closet with a custom system is far faster: our components are manufactured off-site, and our own crew installs a standard closet in about one day.

Can a general contractor build closet shelving too?
Yes, a carpenter or contractor can build shelving on-site, but it's slow, dusty, and often billed by the hour. You also tend to get a basic rod-and-shelf setup rather than a space-maximizing design. A custom closet company designs the interior in 3D first and manufactures it in a shop, which gives you a cleaner install and a smarter layout.

Do I need a contractor if my closet already has walls and a door?
No. If the space already exists, you don't need framing or drywall work, so there's no reason to take on a contractor's timeline and coordination. A dedicated custom closet company can design, build, and install a full custom system without any construction.
